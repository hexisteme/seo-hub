The most common misreading of a subscription billing platform's price page is assuming the listed rate is the whole cost. In practice, card processing fees come out first, and the billing platform's rate is charged separately on top of that. Until you add both layers together, you cannot tell which platform is cheaper.

The number on the pricing page is not the number you pay

Every rate in the table above sits on top of card processing. A subscription billing platform does not replace your payment processor — it orchestrates invoices, proration, dunning, and renewals, and then hands the actual charge to a gateway that takes its own cut. So the real cost of running subscriptions is two rates stacked, not one.

This matters most at the low end. A platform advertising a fraction-of-a-percent rate can still be the more expensive choice once its required monthly floor is included, because that floor is charged whether or not you bill anything that month. Conversely, a pure percentage with no platform fee costs nothing in a slow month and scales linearly in a fast one. Which shape is cheaper depends entirely on your billing volume, not on which number looks smaller.

One thing we could not settle from the public pages: whether Chargebee's platform rate is charged on gross billing value before the gateway's cut, or on what remains after it. The pricing page does not say, and the distinction changes the effective rate. It is listed among the open questions below rather than resolved by guesswork.

Pricing stops being published exactly where it starts to matter

All three vendors publish a rate for the entry tier and stop publishing somewhere above it. Chargebee lists an ingestion ceiling for its top tier without a price. Recurly names a monthly billing-volume minimum for its top tier and describes the rate qualitatively rather than numerically. Stripe Billing states plainly that past a threshold, pricing becomes custom.

This is not an oversight, and it is worth reading as information rather than as an obstacle. It tells you that above a certain size, the rate is negotiated — which means the published rate is a ceiling for a large merchant and a floor for a small one. If your volume is near any of the disclosed thresholds, the useful move is not to compare published rates but to ask all three for a quote and compare those.

Add-ons are priced like separate products, because they are

Revenue recognition, retention tooling, and quote-to-cash are sold alongside the billing platform rather than inside it, and each carries its own floor. A team that assumed revenue recognition came bundled can find the add-on costing more than the billing platform it attaches to — and the add-on floors we could verify are billed annually, so the commitment is a year, not a month.

When comparing platforms, compare the set of modules you will actually turn on. A lower headline rate on the core platform tells you very little if the module you need is priced separately on one vendor and included on another.

FAQ

How much does Chargebee's billing platform charge per month?

Chargebee's Flow product (its current primary billing-platform offering) has no base platform fee on Pay-as-you-go — it charges 0.80% of billing volume. The calculator's own default example, set to $20,000/mo in billing volume, computes that plan's total to $160/mo. The alternative Commit-monthly option instead charges a $99 flat fee plus a lower 0.65% rate.

Does Chargebee offer a free subscription billing plan?

Chargebee's core Flow/Billing platform is not free. One adjacent product is confirmed free under conditions: CPQ Lite is free for an initial batch of quotes. Chargebee's Growth product previously advertised a conditional $0 'Starter' tier, but its price is not published anymore on the current pricing page — only a feature checklist is shown.

How much does Recurly charge for subscription billing?

Recurly's entry-level Starter plan costs $249/month plus 0.9% of billing volume, with an initial tranche of monthly billings excluded from that percentage charge.

What do Recurly's Engage and RevRec add-ons cost on top of the base billing platform?

Both are priced separately from Recurly's core subscription plans: Recurly Engage (churn prediction/retention) starts at $1,600/month billed annually, and Recurly RevRec (revenue recognition) starts at $850/month — neither figure is included in the Starter or All-Access base price.

Is Stripe Billing a separate fee from Stripe's payment processing fees?

Yes. Stripe Billing's Pay-as-you-go rate of 0.7% of Billing volume is charged in addition to Stripe's standard card-processing fee — Billing and payment processing appear as two separate line items, which is the double-charging structure this whole category can obscure.

What billing volume does Stripe Billing's cheapest flat-rate 'Pay monthly' tier cover?

The lowest 'Pay monthly' tier costs $620/month (an 11% discount versus Pay-as-you-go at that volume) and covers up to $100,000 of Billing volume per month, with any volume above that charged an extra 0.67%.

At what point do these subscription billing platforms switch from published percentage pricing to custom enterprise pricing?

Each vendor has its own undisclosed-rate ceiling: Chargebee's top published Flow tier (Enterprise Plus) requires a custom quote once a business exceeds its top listed usage-ingestion tier, Recurly's All-Access plan already requires a $1M/month billing-volume minimum with only a vague 'as low as under 1%' rate disclosed, and Stripe Billing explicitly moves to custom, sales-negotiated pricing above $1,000,000/month in Billing volume.
