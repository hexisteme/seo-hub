PayPal Instant Transfer Fees: The Minimum, the Cap, and When Business Beats Personal

Fact-checked and signed by Seonrye Kim, who checks each figure against the provider's own published pricing page. · Updated 2026-09-04

  • A personal account pays 1.75% of the amount transferred for an Instant Transfer, whether the money moves to a linked bank account or a debit card.
  • A business account pays 1.50% of the amount transferred for that same Instant Transfer, to a bank account or a debit card alike.
  • On a personal account, the Instant Transfer fee stops at a published maximum of $25.00 (25.00 USD), no matter how large the transfer.
  • A business account's Instant Transfer carries a published minimum fee of $0.50 (0.50 USD), and no maximum fee is listed against it.
  • A Standard transfer to a linked bank account costs a personal account nothing — the published fee table lists no fee at all, as long as the transfer does not involve a currency conversion.

PayPal quotes a flat percentage for an Instant Transfer out of your account — 1.75% on a personal account, 1.50% on a business account — but that headline rate is not what most transfers actually cost. PayPal's own published fee table adds a floor under the percentage on a small transfer, a ceiling over it on a personal account's larger one, and a free Standard alternative for anyone willing to wait a few business days instead of getting the money instantly. In dollars, those three rules make a business account's Instant Transfer cheaper than a personal one in the middle of the range, but not at either end of it. It covers only the withdrawal side — moving money out of PayPal to your own bank or card. The separate fee PayPal charges to accept a payment, on the receiving end, is covered on the PayPal fees page.

The published minimum and maximum, not just the percentage

PayPal states the Instant Transfer rate as a flat percentage, but its own fee table adds two more rules on top of it. The first is a floor: PayPal will not charge less than $0.25 on a personal account or $0.50 on a business account, no matter how small the transfer is — below a certain size, the percentage alone would come out under that floor, so the floor is what actually applies. The second rule only appears on a personal account: PayPal will not charge more than $25.00, no matter how large the transfer is. A business account has no published ceiling at all — its 1.50% keeps scaling with the transfer size, with nothing to cap it.

Why business is cheaper in the middle of the range, not at either end

Line the two account types up at the same transfer size and business usually wins: 1.50% is simply a lower rate than 1.75%, so a business account keeps more once the percentage — not the floor or the ceiling — is what decides the fee. That holds from the size where the percentage overtakes the minimum fee up to the size where the personal cap takes over. It stops holding at either extreme. On the smallest transfers, PayPal's minimum fee is what applies, and personal's own minimum happens to be lower than business's. On the largest transfers, personal's fee stops growing altogether once it reaches PayPal's own cap, while business's uncapped rate keeps rising — so past a certain size, a personal account becomes the cheaper way to move the same amount of money.

The free way to move it — if you can wait

PayPal's Standard transfer to a linked bank account carries no fee at all, on either a personal or a business account, as long as the transfer does not involve a currency conversion. Instant Transfer trades that free price for speed. If the timing does not matter, Standard is the one PayPal does not charge for.

How much you can move in one transfer

PayPal also limits how much can move through an Instant Transfer at once, and the limit depends on the account type and the destination. A business account can send up to $25,000.00 to a bank account in a single Instant Transfer, or up to $50,000.00 to a card. A personal account's Instant Transfer to a card is capped at $5,000.00 per transaction. None of these limits are about the fee — they cap how much of a single transfer PayPal will process this way at all, regardless of what it costs.

FAQ

What's the minimum PayPal will charge for an Instant Transfer?

PayPal's own published fee table sets a floor under the percentage: a minimum of $0.25 on a personal account and $0.50 on a business account. On a $10 transfer, the percentage rate alone would fall under that floor, so the minimum is what you actually pay — the transfer nets $9.75 on a personal account and $9.50 on a business account.

Is a business account's Instant Transfer cheaper than a personal account's?

At a $100 transfer, yes — PayPal's business rate of 1.50% beats the personal rate of 1.75%, so a business account nets $98.50 against $98.25 on a personal account. The gap holds at a $1,000 transfer too: $985.00 for business against $982.50 for personal.

Does that mean a business account is always the cheaper way to move money out of PayPal?

No — PayPal's own $25.00 cap on a personal account's Instant Transfer fee has no business-account equivalent. On a $10,000 transfer, a personal account's fee still cannot exceed $25.00, while a business account's uncapped 1.50% rate charges $150.00. A business account's 1.50% catches up to that $25.00 cap at a $1,666.67 transfer — above that size, a personal account is the cheaper way to move the same money.

Is there a way to send money out of PayPal for free?

Yes — PayPal's Standard transfer to a linked bank account has no fee at all, on both a personal and a business account, as long as the transfer does not involve a currency conversion. The trade-off is speed: the Instant Transfer fee is what PayPal charges to skip the wait.

What's the most PayPal can withdraw in a single Instant Transfer?

It depends on the account and the destination. A business account's Instant Transfer to a bank account is capped at $25,000.00 per transaction, and to a card at $50,000.00. A personal account's Instant Transfer to a card is capped at $5,000.00 per transaction.

Before you rely on these numbers

  • PayPal's fee page does not state a per-transaction limit for a personal account's Instant Transfer to a bank account, only to a card, so no personal bank-side limit is shown above.
  • These figures use PayPal's own published rates for a transfer with no currency conversion; a transfer that does involve one carries an additional cost that PayPal prices separately, and that additional cost is not covered here.
  • PayPal's own fee pages state the numbers compared here without explaining why the business rate, minimum fee and card limit differ from the personal ones — only the published figures are compared, not the reasoning behind them.
