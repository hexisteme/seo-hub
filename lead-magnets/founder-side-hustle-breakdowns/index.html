The First 100 Customers Worksheet

A decision tree for choosing where to look for your first 100 customers, plus a channel checklist and a blank ledger to track what actually happens once you start.

There is no universal answer to "where do the first 100 customers come from" — it depends on what you're selling, who you already have access to, and how much time versus money you can spend. This worksheet doesn't tell you the channel. It helps you rule out the ones that don't fit your situation, so you can commit to the ones that do.

Part 1 — The decision tree

Answer each question and follow the branch. You'll usually land on 2–3 candidate channels, not one — that's expected. Run the Part 2 checklist on each candidate before you commit real time to it.

Q1. Do you already have an audience, community, or network that overlaps with your buyer? (a newsletter, a following, a professional network, a Slack/Discord you're active in, past coworkers in the target role)

  • Yes → Start with direct outreach into that existing relationship. It's the cheapest validation you'll ever get, and it tells you fast whether the offer lands.
  • No → Go to Q2.

Q2. Is your buyer a business (B2B) or an individual consumer (B2C)?

  • B2B → Cold outreach, partnerships with adjacent tools/services, and niche communities (industry forums, LinkedIn groups) tend to outperform paid ads early, because B2B buyers research before they trust.
  • B2C → Go to Q3.

Q3. Can your product be demonstrated visually or does it solve a problem people already search for?

  • Visual/demo-able → Short-form content and community posting (where you can show, not just tell) tend to convert attention into interest faster than static ads.
  • Search-driven problem → SEO/content and marketplace or directory listings (wherever your buyer already looks for a solution) are worth testing before ads.

Q4. Do you have budget to spend on acquisition before you have revenue?

  • Yes, some → Small, tightly scoped paid tests (a few channels, capped spend, clear kill criteria) can compress the time it takes to learn what Q1–Q3 already pointed you toward.
  • No → Everything above still works without budget — it just costs time instead. Don't skip to paid ads because organic feels slow; slow and free beats fast and broke for a pre-revenue product.

Note on survivorship bias: public breakdowns of "how I got my first 100 customers" almost never mention the channels that were tried and quietly dropped. This ledger is designed to capture your dropped channels too — a full row of zeroes in the "Customers gained" column is data, not a failure to hide.

Founder & Side-Hustle Breakdowns — Prompt Pack

Ten prompts for Claude or ChatGPT, built to pair with the First 100 Customers Worksheet. Copy the full prompt, fill in the brackets with your own situation, and run it. These are planning and analysis prompts — none of them ask the model to promise an outcome, estimate your revenue, or cite specific numbers from someone else's business.

1. Audience Access Inventory

What it's for: Surfaces every existing relationship or audience you might be underestimating before you look at outside channels.

Help me inventory every existing audience or network I might already have access to for
a new product. Ask me about: past coworkers, professional communities I'm active in,
personal social media following, email lists I control, group chats or Discords/Slacks
I'm a member of (not just ones I own), and any past customers or users from previous
projects.

My product: [one-sentence description]
My background: [paste a few sentences about your work history, communities you're in,
and any past projects]

Based on what I gave you, list the specific pockets of access I have, ranked by how
likely each one is to contain my actual target buyer. Don't invent categories I didn't
mention — only work from what I told you.

Swap: your product description and background.

2. Channel Shortlist Generator

What it's for: Narrows the ten possible channels down to the 2–3 worth testing first, based on your specific situation.

Here is my product and situation: [product description, B2B or B2C, budget available,
time available per week, whether I have an existing audience]

From this list of channels — personal network outreach, existing community
participation, cold outreach/DMs, content/SEO, paid ads, partnerships, marketplace or
directory listings, waitlist/pre-launch, in-person/local, referral loop — recommend the
2-3 most worth testing first for my specific situation, and explain why the others are
lower priority right now (not permanently ruled out, just not first).

Swap: your product/situation details.

3. Cold Outreach Message Drafter

What it's for: Drafts a specific, non-spammy first message for a channel you've chosen — not a generic template.

I'm reaching out to [describe the specific person or type of person] about [my product,
one sentence]. Here's what I know about why this specific person might care: [your
actual reasoning — a problem they have, something you noticed about them, a mutual
connection].

Draft a short outreach message (under 80 words) that: references something specific and
true about them (not a fake personalization), states clearly what I'm offering without
over-selling, and asks a low-commitment question rather than pushing for a sale
immediately. Avoid hype language and avoid implying urgency that doesn't exist.

Swap: the recipient description and your real reasoning for reaching out.

4. Community Warm-Intro Finder

What it's for: Helps you find a non-spammy way to bring up your product inside a community you're already a genuine member of.

I'm a genuine, active member of this community: [describe it — what it's about, how
you participate, how long you've been there]. I want to mention my product there without
it reading as a drive-by ad.

My product: [one-sentence description]

Suggest 3 different ways I could naturally bring this up — for example, answering a
question that's already being asked, sharing a specific lesson from building it, or
asking for feedback rather than pitching. For each, write a short example post. Flag if
any of my 3 options risks reading as spam to an existing community, and tell me why.

Swap: your community description and product.

5. Landing Page First-Line Generator

What it's for: Drafts the first line of value-proposition copy for a pre-launch or early landing page.

My product: [what it does, in your own words]
Who it's for: [specific description of the buyer]
The problem it solves: [the actual pain point, described concretely]

Write 5 different first-line headline options for a landing page. Each should state the
outcome or problem solved, not just describe the product's features. Do not include any
specific numbers, testimonials, or claims I have not given you — if a headline needs a
stat to feel complete, leave a bracketed placeholder like [X] instead of inventing one.

Swap: your product, audience, and problem description.

6. Weekly Customer Acquisition Retro

What it's for: Turns your ledger entries from the worksheet into a short retro you can act on.

Here are my customer acquisition ledger entries from this week: [paste your ledger rows
— date, channel, action, customers gained, cost, notes]

Summarize: which channel(s) produced the clearest signal (positive or negative) this
week, what pattern (if any) is showing up across entries, and one specific experiment to
try next week based only on this data. Do not extrapolate a growth rate or revenue
projection from this — a few data points is not enough for that, and say so if I ask.

Swap: your actual ledger rows.

7. Partnership Pitch Drafter

What it's for: Drafts a short co-marketing or partnership pitch to a complementary (non-competing) business.

I want to propose a partnership to [describe the business — what they do, why they're
complementary, not competing with me].

My product: [one-sentence description]
What I could offer them: [be specific — a mention to my audience, a revenue share, a
free integration, something concrete]

Draft a short partnership pitch email (under 150 words) that leads with what's in it for
them, states clearly what I'm asking for, and makes it easy to say yes to a small first
step (not a big commitment).

Swap: the target business and what you can realistically offer.

8. Pre-Revenue Pricing Page Draft

What it's for: Drafts a first-pass pricing page for a product that doesn't have customers yet, without inventing social proof.

My product: [description]
My best guess at who pays and what they'd pay: [your reasoning, even if uncertain]

Draft a simple pricing page with 1-3 tiers. Do not include customer counts, testimonials,
"trusted by" logos, or usage statistics — I don't have any yet, and a fabricated one
would be dishonest even as placeholder text. Leave those sections out entirely rather
than filling them with fake numbers.

Swap: your product and pricing reasoning.

9. Failure Signal Diagnostic

What it's for: Helps you tell the difference between a channel that's actually not working and one that just needs more attempts.

I've been testing this channel: [channel name] for [amount of time/number of attempts].
Here's what happened: [paste your actual results — even "nothing happened" counts]

Help me reason through whether this is a real failure signal (the channel doesn't fit my
product/audience) or just insufficient sample size (I haven't tried enough times to know
yet). Ask me clarifying questions if you need more detail before answering. Don't tell me
to "just keep going" without a specific reason tied to what I told you.

Swap: the channel, your effort so far, and actual results.

10. First-100 Ledger Pattern Analyzer

What it's for: Looks across your full ledger (not just one week) for patterns once you have enough entries to matter.

Here is my full first-100-customers ledger so far: [paste all rows]

Looking across all entries: which channel(s) show a consistent pattern (positive or
negative), which show too few data points to conclude anything yet, and what's one
honest observation about my own effort distribution (am I spreading attempts too thin
across channels, or concentrating on one before it's proven out)? Be direct if the
data doesn't support a clear conclusion yet — don't manufacture a trend from a handful
of rows.

Swap: your full ledger.
