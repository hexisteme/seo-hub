The headline rate is rarely the rate you pay

Every row below the first one is an addition to the headline percentage, and they stack. A domestic card typed in by hand at Stripe is not 2.9% — it is 2.9% plus 0.5%. A card issued abroad, paid in a currency the processor has to convert, picks up two more additions at once.

This matters most if your customers are not all in one country. On the international-card line the three are close together — 1.5%, 1.50% and 1.5%. On the currency-conversion line they are not close at all: Stripe publishes 1%, PayPal publishes a 4.00% spread, and Square publishes nothing. For a business selling across borders, that single undisclosed cell is worth more than the entire headline-rate comparison above it.

How to reduce what you pay

Three levers come straight out of the table, and none of them require negotiating with anyone:

  • Stop keying cards in by hand. At Stripe that alone is 0.5% on every affected transaction. A keyed-in surcharge is not published by PayPal or Square in the sources checked here, so if you process this way, ask them directly and get it in writing.
  • Charge in your customer's currency only if you know the conversion cost. The gap between 1% and a 4.00% spread is larger than the gap between any two headline rates compared here.
  • Check whether a paid plan is cheaper than the free one. Square is the clear case: the Free plan's online rate is 3.3% while Plus and Premium drop it to 2.9%. Whether that trade is worth $49/mo depends entirely on your volume — and that is arithmetic you can do, not a judgement call.

What you will not find here is a claim about which processor is cheapest overall. That depends on your transaction size, your mix of domestic and international cards, and your volume, and any page that answers it without knowing those things is guessing.

Why the fixed fee decides small transactions

The percentage dominates conversation, but on small tickets the flat fee dominates the bill. Stripe and Square both charge 30¢ per transaction; PayPal charges 0.49 USD. On a large order that difference is noise. On a small one it is not — and it is the reason micro-transaction businesses read the fixed-fee row before the percentage row. The exact crossover depends on your average order value, which is arithmetic only you can do.

FAQ

How much does Stripe charge per online transaction?

Stripe's Standard plan charges 2.9% + 30 cents per successful online transaction for domestic US cards. There is no monthly or setup fee on this plan.

How much does PayPal charge for online sales?

PayPal Checkout charges 3.49% plus a fixed fee (0.49 USD for transactions received in US dollars) for standard domestic commercial transactions. This is higher than Stripe's or Square's base online rate.

How much does Square charge for online payments?

Square charges 3.3% + 30 cents per online or invoice payment on its free plan, dropping to 2.9% + 30 cents on its paid Plus and Premium plans. Its payments API rate is 2.9% + 30 cents on every plan.

Which processor is cheapest on international payments: Stripe, PayPal, or Square?

Stripe and Square both add a flat 1.5% surcharge when the card is issued outside the US, while PayPal adds 1.50% on top of its already-higher 3.49% base rate for an international commercial transaction — a condition PayPal defines by the transaction rather than by where the card was issued. On the surcharge percentage alone, Stripe and Square are tied and both are lower than PayPal's combined effective rate.

Do Stripe, PayPal, or Square charge a currency conversion fee?

Stripe discloses a 1% fee when currency conversion is required, and PayPal discloses a 4.00% currency conversion spread (or another disclosed amount). Square's official fees page does not publish a specific currency-conversion figure, so that comparison point is unconfirmed for Square.

Do Stripe, PayPal, and Square charge monthly or setup fees?

Stripe's Standard plan has no setup or monthly fee, and Square's Free plan also has no monthly subscription cost (Square's Plus and Premium tiers do add a $49/mo or $149/mo per-location fee). PayPal's standard PayPal Checkout monthly/setup fee status could not be confirmed from an explicit statement on its official fees page.
